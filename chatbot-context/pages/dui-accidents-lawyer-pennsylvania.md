# Being Charged with a DUI Accident with Injuries in Pennsylvania

A car accident with injuries is always a frightening situation. But if you happened to be intoxicated at the time of the accident and were charged with DUI, you face serious penalties that could damage the rest of your life.

If you were charged with DUI and injuries were involved, you can also be charged with [**aggravated assault**](https://shragerdefense.com/violent-crimes-attorneys/aggravated-assault/), which is a felony. If a death occurred due to the accident, you could be charged with [**vehicular homicide**](https://shragerdefense.com/criminal-lawyers-pittsburgh/vehicular-homicide/).

If you are convicted of DUI Aggravated Assault, you face up to ten years in jail and a $25,000 fine. A DUI Homicide conviction is a mandatory three years in prison and a $25,000 fine for each victim killed in the accident.

Having a felony on your permanent record can have dire consequences to your job, future employment, and reputation – for the rest of your life.

## **PENALTIES FOR A DUI ACCIDENT WITH INJURIES**

Penalties for a DUI in Pennsylvania are highly dependent on the **[BAC (Blood Alcohol Content) level](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/pennsylvania-dui-laws/)** at the time of the arrest.

A typical [**first offense DUI**](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/first-time-dui-pennsylvania/) with no accident or injury occurring, carries a maximum penalty of a $5,000 fine, 6 months in jail, a 12 month license suspension and a misdemeanor on your record.

### AGGRAVATED ASSAULT BY VEHICLE – CHARGES AND PENALTIES

If an accident occurs and any person (other than the driver) receives “serious bodily injury” in the accident, the DUI driver could be charged with aggravated assault.

Serious bodily injury is defined as, “any bodily injury which creates a substantial risk of death or causes serious, permanent disfigurement or protracted loss or impairment of the function of any bodily member or organ.” In order to be convicted, guilt must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt that the serious bodily injury was directly caused by the DUI.

**The penalty if convicted carries a maximum sentence of 10 years in jail and a maximum $25,000 fine.**

### OTHER DUI CHARGES AND PENALTIES

Even if another party to the accident did not receive a serious bodily injury, but did receive some lesser injury, you could face additional charges that could increase your sentencing.

Some such charges include [**simple assault**](https://shragerdefense.com/violent-crimes-attorneys/simple-assault/) and recklessly endangering another person. The more charges you face, the harsher your penalties upon conviction.

Pennsylvania takes drunk driving very seriously and therefore offenders are often charged the maximum penalty allowed under the law.

### **OTHER CONSEQUENCES OF A DUI CONVICTION**

It can be very stressful to have the possibility of prison looming over you. If you are convicted and receive the maximum penalty, both you and your family will suffer financially and emotionally. Your insurance rates could increase, you may have potential hospital bills to pay, the inability to drive to and from work could cause you to lose your job entirely.

Your reputation will also suffer. With a felony on your permanent record, you could struggle to find employment in the future and not be eligible for financial loans for school, cars, houses, etc.

**You can also be held responsible in civil court for injuries sustained by another due to your DUI.** This means you could potentially be responsible to the victim(s), or the victim’s family, for certain civil wrongdoings such as emotional damages, lost wages, medical expenses, pain and suffering and property damage. In addition to any criminal fines you may have to pay, you could owe a far greater sum to an injured party. This could be financially crippling to you and your family.
