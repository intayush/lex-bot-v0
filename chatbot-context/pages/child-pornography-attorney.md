## SENTENCING AND PENALTIES FOR A CHILD PORNOGRAPHY CONVICTION

**Mandatory minimum sentences for federal child pornography include five years of prison time&#x20;**&#x66;or any of the following offenses:

* **Receipt of Child Pornography**
* **Distribution of Child Pornography**
* **Possession of Child Pornography with Intent to Distribute or Sell**
* **Transportation of Child Pornography**
* **Production of Child Pornography**

Photographing child pornography or causing a child to participate in the making of pornography is a felony with a penalty of up to ten years in prison and a maximum $25,000 fine.

If you possess or distribute child pornography, you could be convicted of a felony and sentenced to up to seven years in prison and up to $15,000 in fines.

Circulating or spreading sexually explicit material to a minor is also a felony and punishable with prison time and hefty fines. Other lewd acts toward children may be considered misdemeanors and if convicted could sentence you to up to five years in prison and $10,000 in fines.

Recently, Pennsylvania passed House Bill 321, which toughens the penalties for possessing child pornography. The guidelines are based upon the child’s age, the number of images in possession, and whether the images involve indecent conduct with the child. Read PA House Bill 321 on Sentencing for Offenses Involving Sexual Abuse of Children **[here](http://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/li/uconsCheck.cfm?yr=2013\&sessInd=0\&act=105)**.

In addition to these penalties, you may be required to register as a sex offender for the rest of your life under the **[Sexual Offender Registration and Notification Act (SORNA) / Megan’s Law in Pennsylvania. ](https://shragerdefense.com/failure-to-register-sex-offender-pennsylvania/)**

## Federal Charges

Possession and distribution of child pornography is also prosecuted as a [**federal crime**](/federal-crime-attorneys "federal crimes lawyer"). The Prosecutorial Remedies and Other Tools to End the Exploitation of Children Today (PROTECT) Act of 2003 illegalizes the production, distribution, or possession of any type of child pornography. It is also a federal crime to use a computer to ship, transport, receive, distribute, or reproduce for distribution child pornography or to solicit pornographic images of children. You can read more about federal charges of child pornography on [**The United States Department of Justice website.**](http://www.justice.gov/criminal/ceos/citizensguide/citizensguide_porn.html)

Being charged with a federal crime is a much more complex affair and will take an experienced attorney to tackle all the paperwork and prepare a strategic defense.
