# **![dui on prescription meds](https://shragerdefense.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/prescription_drugs-300x253.jpg)DUI – PRESCRIPTION MEDICATION IN PENNSYLVANIA**

DUI is often associated with alcohol, but people can also be charged with DUI while taking prescription medication or even certain over-the-counter medication. Whether the prescription is legal from a pharmacy or whether it is an illegally prescribed drug does not matter – if an officer finds your driving to be impaired or if [**an accident has occurred&#x20;**](https://shragerdefense.com/dui-accidents-lawyer-pennsylvania/)while on prescription meds, you could be charged with a DUI.

[**Driving Under the Influence of a Controlled Substance / Drugs**](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/dui-drugs/), or DUI-D, in Pennsylvania is a very serious offense. PA follows a “per se” law, which means any drug on a specific list is an automatic DUI charge in the Highest Impairment Tier (a BAC of over .16%). This holds the same penalty as being caught driving while high on marijuana, heroin, cocaine or any other illegal [**controlled substance**](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/dui-drugs/).

**This means that a first DUI could mean a one-year license suspension, up to six months in jail (a minimum of 72 hours in jail), and a fine up to $5,000.**

Regardless of whether the drug was obtained illegally, prescribed to you by a doctor, or something you bought at the local drug store, you will face the highest penalties under the law.

But just because you have been arrested for a DUI does not necessarily mean you will be convicted.

## **PENALTIES OF A DUI DRUGS CONVICTION IN PENNSYLVANIA**

If you are arrested for DUI Drugs, you will be charged in the Commonwealth’s Highest Impairment Tier, which is the same as having a BAC of .16 or higher. This is regardless of the amount of the drug found in your system.

In its effort to eradicate impaired driving, Pennsylvania has adopted very strict guidelines for driving with the use of a controlled substance. A controlled substance includes those you may have been prescribed to use on a daily basis, for a number of ailments, including, but not limited to: Xanax, Ambien, Prozac, Oxycodone and Valium.

**If convicted of having any amount of these drugs in your system while operating a vehicle,** **you could receive:**

* **A minimum of 72 hours and a maximum of 6 months in jail;**
* **A misdemeanor on your permanent record;**
* **A fine of $1,000 to $5,000;**
* **A one-year license suspension;**
* **Mandatory substance abuse highway safety school.**

This penalty is for a [**first DUI offense**](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/first-time-dui-pennsylvania/); the penalties increase if you have had a [**second DUI**](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/second-dui/) or receive a [**subsequent DUI**](https://shragerdefense.com/pittsburgh-dui-attorney/multiple-duis-in-pennsylvania/).

If your license is suspended, you will have no way to drive to and from your place of employment. Or worse, you could be fired and seen as no longer trustworthy. The fines and increased insurance premiums you are likely to encounter could be crippling.
